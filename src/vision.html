Vision

The largest unbuilt surface in software is the one you talk to.

Orinda Labs is building the intelligence layer for conversations that happen between real people, in real places — and it can only be built with consent.

Almost every system you use was built on things people typed: queries, forms, messages, clicks. It is an extraordinarily rich record, and it is also a record of people alone with a screen. The moment a person turns to another person and speaks, software stops being able to help.

That is a strange place for the industry to have ended up, because the spoken moment is the higher-signal one. A person who says out loud, to your face, what they are trying to do has told you more in forty seconds than a month of their browsing ever will. And unlike browsing, they knew they were telling you.

Today, the tools built on those moments treat the person as a row to extract. The value accrues to one side and evaporates the moment the event ends.

We think the durable version is two-sided. The person who spoke gets an AI that remembers and acts on what they cared about, wherever they go next. The person who listened — the exhibitor, the host, the organizer — gets an AI that captures the room and follows through. Both sides get something out of the same moment, which is the only arrangement in which both sides keep showing up.

What that produces over time is something no amount of scraping can manufacture: a consented map of real-world intent — expressed in person, owned and controllable by the people in it. Not inferred from behaviour. Not bought from a broker. Given, deliberately, by someone standing in front of you.

Why events first

Events and venues are where we started, and they are not the whole plan. They are the best possible first market for this: the consent is unambiguous because the two people are standing together and one of them is asking; the conversation is unusually high-signal because nobody attends an event by accident; and the follow-through is worth real money to the host, so the software has to earn its place immediately.

Everything we learn making conversation legible in a loud hall — the capture, the consent flow, the understanding, the acting on it — is the same machinery wherever people meet in person. Raplo Capture is one application of it. It is not the shape of the company.

Our consent commitment

None of this works without consent, so consent is not something we bolt on afterwards. A person’s details and conversation enter our software only when that person has explicitly agreed. That holds for every product on this page, shipped or in development.

The person the data is about controls it and can have it deleted. We do not capture people who have not agreed to be captured, we do not buy or sell personal data, and we do not build profiles of people from sources they did not consent to.

How we handle personal information, and how to exercise your rights over it, is set out in our privacy policy. Questions go to privacy@orindalabs.com.
